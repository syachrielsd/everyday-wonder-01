# Screening — Everyday Wonder, Two Days to Find the Line

Judge criteria, in order: (1) small moment of wonder in everyday life, single sentence;
(2) fresh angle, the phrasing itself the surprise; (3) surprise lands in one read,
single clear sentence; (4) grounded in something real and specific; (5) every word
working, no filler, no worn phrase. Scores 1–5 per criterion, in that order.

Avoided the brief's own examples (lift door / stranger's song, kettle click, puddle
holding the sky) and hunted unnoticed mechanisms instead.

## Candidates

### 1. Receipt — 116 chars — 5 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 4 = 22
"The corner-shop receipt outlived the groceries and kept whispering Tuesday's prices from my pocket all week."
Reason: "outlived the groceries" is a fresh angle, but "whispering" leans lyrical and softens the snap.

### 2. Revolving door — 102 chars — 5 / 4 / 5 / 5 / 4 = 23
"I pushed the revolving door and for one slow quarter-turn the whole street belonged to me alone."
Reason: "one slow quarter-turn" is specific and the ownership flip lands in one read; "belonged to me alone" is a touch grand.

### 3. Bus-stop umbrella — 118 chars — 5 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 3 = 21
"At the bus stop, a stranger's umbrella tilted over my shoulder without a word, and the rain suddenly had manners."
Reason: "the rain suddenly had manners" is charming, but "without a word" is filler and the setup runs long.

### 4. Fridge light — 117 chars — 4 / 3 / 4 / 4 / 3 = 18
"Every night the fridge light comes on like a tiny sunrise just for me, and the milk glows like it knows a secret."
Reason: "tiny sunrise" is a worn image; the wonder is asserted rather than discovered in the phrasing.

### 5. Crosswalk — 121 chars — 5 / 5 / 4 / 5 / 4 = 23
"The crosswalk man turned green and twenty strangers crossed together, trusting a little lit figure with their lives."
Reason: the absurd trust in a pictogram is a genuinely fresh angle; "little lit figure" carries the surprise.

### 6. Vending machine — 119 chars — 5 / 4 / 5 / 5 / 4 = 23
"The vending machine dropped my chocolate with a thunk, like a small machine somewhere had finally kept a promise."
Reason: "finally kept a promise" reframes the thunk well, but the machine-as-person move is a familiar one.

### 7. Elevator mirror — 112 chars — 5 / 4 / 5 / 5 / 4 = 23
"The elevator mirror caught me mid-yawn, and for three floors I studied a stranger who happened to be me."
Reason: "a stranger who happened to be me" is a clean turn; the mirror-as-stranger angle is widely used, though.

### 8. Escalator handrail — 126 chars — 5 / 5 / 4 / 5 / 5 = 24 ★ WINNER
"The escalator handrail moves a fraction slower than the steps, so my hand keeps arriving late to where I already stand."
Reason: the handrail really does drift out of sync with the steps, a true detail almost nobody has named; the paradox of arriving late to where you already stand is carried entirely by the phrasing, every word earns its place.

### 9. ATM — 110 chars — 4 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 4 = 20
"The ATM counted my money out loud in crisp little clicks, as if the machine were proud of its arithmetic."
Reason: "proud of its arithmetic" is sweet but the angle is mild; the wonder stays on the surface.

### 10. Washing machine — 120 chars — 5 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 4 = 22
"Through the round window the socks tumbled like a tiny grey sea, and I stood watching weather I had made myself."
Reason: "weather I had made myself" is a lovely close, but "tiny grey sea" is a familiar simile shape.

### 11. Key and lock — 114 chars — 4 / 3 / 5 / 5 / 4 = 21
"My key turned and the lock clicked open a fraction before I expected, as if the door had been listening for me."
Reason: one clean read and well grounded, but "the door had been listening" is a trope the reader has met.

### 12. Supermarket scanner — 118 chars — 5 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 3 = 20
"The scanner beeped at each apple, and for a second the groceries sang their prices in a small electronic choir."
Reason: "electronic choir" is cute but the singing-groceries image is worn, and "for a second" is filler.

## Winner

**#8 — Escalator handrail (24/25).** Highest on the deciding criteria: the freshest
angle (criterion 2) and the tightest wording (criterion 5), grounded in a real,
checkable detail — escalator handrails genuinely run a fraction out of sync with
the steps.

Runner-up: **#5 — Crosswalk (23/25)**, also 5 on angle, but its surprise ("trusting a
little lit figure") is stated rather than enacted in the phrasing, and it loses to
#8 on criterion 5.

## Revision note

First draft of #8 carried "already" ("where I already stand"). The revise cycle
tightened it: "already" is an adverb doing no work — the paradox is already in
"arriving late to where I stand". Final sentence in main.ts:

"The escalator handrail moves a fraction slower than the steps, so my hand keeps arriving late to where I stand."
